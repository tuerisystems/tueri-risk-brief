Welcome to the Tueri Systems Risk Brief for Friday, October thirtieth. Operational clarity when conditions change. A few minutes on what is happening, what can be confirmed, and what has changed. Digitally narrated. Two tankers were attacked in the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday. Dammam flights were disrupted again as the Houthis claim new strikes on Saudi Arabia. Spain's rail replacement plan has slipped. And we have a correction on Haiti. Here's the detail.

Shipping first. Confirmed by UK Maritime Trade Operations: on Thursday a tanker leaving the Strait of Hormuz was struck by an unknown projectile, with no injuries reported. Hours later, a second outbound tanker caught fire. A verified update says a small boat attached two explosive devices to its hull. The fire is out, the crew is safe, and the ship is under way. Who carried out the attacks has not been established. The vessel fire reported in the strait early Thursday local time has since been identified by regional authorities as a dhow involved in a security incident. It sank, and the crew was evacuated. The Joint Maritime Information Center's latest weekly assessment, issued before both tanker attacks, rates the threat in the Strait of Hormuz as severe and in the wider Arabian Gulf as substantial. Tueri suggests that operators with ships in the strait review hull watch and transit timing with their security providers now.

Saudi Arabia. Claim only: the Houthis say they carried out six attacks on Thursday, including on airports in Riyadh and Dammam and on oil facilities. Saudi Arabia has not confirmed them. Reported: the Saudi-led coalition says it intercepted missiles aimed at the east and at Riyadh, and on Friday a drone in the Mecca region, which the Houthis deny launching. Flightradar twenty-four showed flights holding away from Dammam early Friday local time. At about five thirty-five a.m. Central, about ten percent of today's Dammam flights were cancelled. At Riyadh's main airport, about fifteen percent were cancelled, but today's schedule is about a third smaller than yesterday's, so the figures are not comparable. On Ras Tanura, the Joint Maritime Information Center records a fire at the export terminal on Tuesday. It says Saudi authorities attributed it to drone debris; the Houthi missile claim remains unconfirmed.

Spain. Reported, per the state rail operator: its replacement service for the closed Madrid to Andalusia high-speed line will not start before Tuesday, November third, after the train drivers' union required notice for schedule changes. The line remains closed with no reopening date.

Borders and health. Confirmed: the amended US Ebola attestation order, covering crew as well as passengers, was published in the Federal Register today. It applies from eleven fifty-nine p.m. Eastern on Tuesday through December eighteenth. In Nigeria, the disease control center's own protocols, now read directly, say healthy travelers who have been in Congo, Uganda, South Sudan, Burundi or Rwanda are monitored daily for twenty-one days, and anyone with symptoms goes to institutional quarantine. These measures are not new. An undated guideline from the same center describes a twenty-one day self-quarantine for arriving passengers, so whether that covers all arrivals is not clear.

Operations check. A correction on Haiti. Yesterday we said the Digicel internet outage was still in place as of early Wednesday. That was true then, but Georgia Tech's measurements show Digicel's signal returned to pre-outage levels from about nine thirty a.m. Central on Wednesday, nearly a day before our last episode. We should have caught that. We found no Digicel restoration notice. Moscow's mobile internet limits were scheduled to end Thursday. We found no extension, though media report some outages continuing. Power: in Andhra Pradesh, state figures carried by media show about ninety percent of villages hit by Arnab have power back. Labor: Italian media report the government ordered transport to keep running during today's general strike, so the rail and air walkout warned of yesterday was called off. British advice still mentions disruption, so check with your operator. In France, the US government warns of protests today and Saturday.
