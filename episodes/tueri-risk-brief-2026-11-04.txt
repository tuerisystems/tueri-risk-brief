Tueri Systems Daily Risk Brief

Welcome to the Tueri Systems Risk Brief for Wednesday, November fourth. Operational clarity when conditions change. A few minutes on what is happening, what can be confirmed, and what has changed. Digitally narrated. Another tanker was struck off northern Oman, and Iran's Revolutionary Guard claims four tankers hit mines near the Strait of Hormuz. The remnants of Cyclone Murasu are still bringing extremely heavy rain to southern India. And in Saudi Arabia, the US security alert stands, with Riyadh flights still being cancelled. Here's the detail.

Gulf shipping. Confirmed by UK Maritime Trade Operations: late on Tuesday, a tanker about fourteen nautical miles off Oman's northern tip was struck by two unknown projectiles in the engine room. No casualties or environmental impact were reported. Also confirmed by UKMTO, citing the Indian government: an Indian crewed dhow was attacked while sailing out through the Strait of Hormuz on Monday. Oman's navy rescued the surviving crew. Trade press reports one crew member was killed. UKMTO published that warning about a day after the attack; reporting can lag. Separately, Iran's Revolutionary Guard says four tankers that ignored its rules hit mines in the strait and are burning. That is a claim only. The vessels have not been identified, and as of this brief UKMTO has issued no warning matching it. The latest joint maritime threat update, issued before these incidents, rates the Strait of Hormuz severe and the Gulf of Oman moderate. Tuesday's tanker strike was on the Gulf of Oman side. Tueri suggests operators with voyages near the strait review routing against the current assessment, and plan for possible escalation without assuming it.

India. Confirmed by the India Meteorological Department: what is left of Cyclone Murasu has weakened into a low pressure area over Telangana and coastal Andhra Pradesh. Extremely heavy rain was recorded there in the twenty-four hours to Wednesday morning, and more very heavy rain is possible today. Reported, per state disaster officials quoted by Indian media: two people have died, more than twelve thousand people are in relief centres, more than two hundred mobile towers were affected, and more than thirty-five trains were cancelled. Power companies say supply is largely restored in one coastal district; that is the operators' account, and some areas remain without power. Tueri suggests organizations with people in the region confirm staff welfare, and check flooding and road conditions before any movement.

Saudi Arabia. Confirmed: the US State Department on Tuesday restated Monday's US Mission security alert, warning of potential attacks on airports, possible airspace closures, and flight cancellations. It is not a new warning. The ordered departure of some US staff and the US travel advisory are unchanged since October twenty-third. At about two thirty p.m. local time today, Flightradar twenty-four showed about fifteen percent of Riyadh flights cancelled. Yesterday's count used a different base, so no trend can be drawn. Tueri suggests travelers keep flexible plans and confirm each flight with the airline before leaving for the airport.

Operations check. Borders and health: the US Ebola attestation rule has been in force since eleven fifty-nine p.m. Eastern on Tuesday. CDC shows no new change to the rule. Travelers who do not attest will be denied boarding. Nigeria's health portal confirms a pre-boarding declaration for all arrivals; a reported twenty-one day follow-up is still not confirmed with the authority. Britain's Foreign Office updated its Madagascar advice: arrivals must declare recent travel history and may be screened for Ebola. It does not say when that began. Aviation: Warsaw's second airport resumed operations on Tuesday after a bomb threat closure, its operator says. Infrastructure: Cloudflare measured internet traffic in Ukraine's Zhytomyr region down about fifty percent from Tuesday evening, after damage to energy facilities cut power. Security, reported per Reuters: Latvia says two drones violated its airspace, one believed Russian and one Ukrainian. Maritime: UKMTO has reclassified last week's boarding of a large fishing vessel off the Horn of Africa as a hijack; authorities fear it may support pirate attacks. Hazards, reported per Philippine media: Mayon volcano remains at alert level three.
