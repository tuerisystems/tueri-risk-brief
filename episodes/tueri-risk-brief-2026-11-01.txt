Nigeria. Confirmed by the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control: two suspected Ebola cases reported in Yola, in Adamawa State, were isolated, and samples tested negative after two rounds of testing. The US Embassy in Abuja says all international arrivals are being screened, and could face quarantine or self-isolation of at least twenty-one days. The embassy relays that as a Nigerian measure; we have not found Nigerian text on who is quarantined. Tueri suggests travelers complete Nigeria's health declaration form before departure and allow time for screening on arrival.

Galapagos. Confirmed by Britain's Foreign Office, updated overnight: the Galapagos regional government declared an emergency after flooding on Santa Cruz island, with road closures, power cuts and disruption to travel to Baltra airport. The US Embassy advises avoiding the road between Puerto Ayora and the highlands. Reported, per Ecuadorian officials quoted in local media: Baltra suspended flights for several hours on Saturday and commercial flights resumed that afternoon; one person died and two are missing; about two thousand people are affected; and piped water in Puerto Ayora is suspended. Some visitor sites are closed. Tueri suggests confirming flights, transfers and accommodation directly with airlines and tour operators before travel this week.

Moscow. Confirmed on the US Embassy's website: on Saturday it warned of a credible threat of a mass casualty event at a large museum park in southern Moscow, and advised avoiding the site that day. Reported, via Russian media: the security service said it was acting on the information, the park was evacuated and closed, and no incident was reported. Local media report the park reopened on Sunday. The US advises against all travel to Russia, and that advice is unchanged.

Horn of Africa. UKMTO published the warning about a day later. Its map places the area off the Horn of Africa, in the Indian Ocean. UKMTO advises vessels to transit with caution and follow best management practices.
