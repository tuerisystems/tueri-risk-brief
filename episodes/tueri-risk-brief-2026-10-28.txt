Welcome to the Tueri Systems Risk Brief for Wednesday, October twenty-eighth. Operational clarity when conditions change. A few minutes on what is happening, what can be confirmed, and what has changed. Digitally narrated. Cyclone Arnab has made landfall in India and is weakening. Saudi Arabia reports more interceptions, including over Riyadh. Gulf shipping has new guidance on the US blockade of Iran. And more countries have added Ebola entry measures. Here's the detail.

India first. Reported, citing the India Meteorological Department: Cyclone Arnab crossed the Andhra Pradesh coast early Wednesday local time, Tuesday evening Central, as a very severe cyclonic storm. It has since weakened and is forecast to keep weakening inland. Reported: officials say at least three people have died and about fifty-seven thousand are in relief camps. Indian media report flights suspended at three Andhra Pradesh airports, dozens of trains cancelled, and power only partly restored. Teams on that coast should confirm road, rail and airport status before moving.

Saudi Arabia. Reported: the Saudi-led coalition says it intercepted three ballistic missiles aimed at the Eastern Province and two over Riyadh. Saudi civil defense says falling debris in Riyadh damaged a building, a construction site and a vehicle. Alerts were later reported around Jeddah and Yanbu. Claim only: the Houthis say a missile hit the Ras Tanura refinery and caused major fires. Saudi Arabia has not confirmed that. Britain's Foreign Office has updated its advice for Saudi Arabia and more than a dozen other countries in the region, and now says the risk of sudden escalation is high. At Riyadh's main airport, Flightradar twenty-four data at about five thirty a.m. Central showed about seventeen percent of today's flights cancelled, against twenty-three percent at the same time yesterday on a similar schedule. One day's change is not a trend. It remains a partial reopening with observed disruption.

Shipping. Confirmed: the Joint Maritime Information Center has issued an advisory note on the US blockade of Iranian ports. The blockade itself is not new. US naval forces say vessels moving between neutral ports in the Gulf of Oman and the Strait of Hormuz may be questioned, visited and searched. Ships should keep a radio watch, follow US directions, have signed cargo and port documents ready, and plan extra transit time. Vessels that do not need to transit are advised to stay well clear. The center's latest weekly assessment rates the threat in the Strait of Hormuz as severe. Reported: Iran's new strait authority says vessels it links to a South Korean tanker operator face legal action from November first. A Korean shipowners' group disputes that link. No new UK Maritime Trade Operations incident warning was found this cycle. That does not mean the threat has eased.

Borders and health. Confirmed: Nigeria's Centre for Disease Control now requires every international arrival, including Nigerian citizens and crew, to complete a health declaration before boarding, with temperature screening on arrival. Reported, per the US Embassy in Abuja: arrivals could face quarantine or self-isolation of at least twenty-one days. Reported, per Britain's Foreign Office: El Salvador now requires a twenty-one-day quarantine for travelers who were in eight African countries, including Kenya, Uganda and Congo, in the previous twenty-one days. Already in force and unchanged: the US limits on travel from Congo, Uganda and South Sudan, and the US attestation rule from November third. Tueri suggests checking travel history against every country on a route, not only the first.

In Bangladesh, a national grid fault cut power to eastern Dhaka and parts of the southeast on Tuesday, and Cloudflare saw traffic fall by up to a quarter. The grid operator says power was restored within about two hours; recovery for all users was not independently measured. Moscow's announced mobile internet limits run through Thursday. Power: Sudan's southeastern grid is not confirmed restored. Hazards: Nepal's authorities warn of heavy rain and snow from Thursday through Saturday. Other domains as above.
