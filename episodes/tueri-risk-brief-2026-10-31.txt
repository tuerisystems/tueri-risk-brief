Shipping first. Confirmed by UK Maritime Trade Operations: on Friday evening local time, a vessel about forty nautical miles off Iran's coast, inside the Arabian Gulf and well west of the Strait of Hormuz, was hit on the port side by an unknown projectile. It caught fire; the crew is safe. A verified report published Friday adds a crude tanker struck while leaving the strait on Wednesday, reported two days after it occurred, so the known incident record has grown. Reported: Oman's maritime security center says it evacuated twenty-five crew from a burning tanker in the strait on Friday. Claim only: Iran's Revolutionary Guard navy says four tankers on what it calls unauthorized routes were stopped, some after hitting mines. Reported: US Central Command denies the mine claims. It is not clear whether any of these are the same ships. The Joint Maritime Information Center rates the threat as severe in the Strait of Hormuz and substantial in the wider Arabian Gulf, where Friday's projectile attack fell. Tueri suggests operators in the Gulf, not only in the strait, review hull watch and routing with their security providers.

Honduras. The US Embassy issued a security alert about a reported threat to bridges and access routes around Puerto Cortés early Saturday and advised avoiding the area this weekend. Reported: Honduras' security minister said there is no evidence of a credible plot and it may be a false alarm. Port authorities raised the port's security level, and terminals are reported to be operating. Tueri suggests confirming routes with freight agents before moving cargo or staff there this weekend.

Philippines. Reported, via the US Embassy and media: the national volcano institute raised Mayon volcano to alert level three after an explosive eruption on Friday and recommends evacuation of the six kilometer danger zone. We found no reported airline cancellations.

Ethiopia and Kenya. Confirmed by British travel advice updated Friday: violence in the Ethiopian border town of Moyale has affected the border area, with stray gunfire reported on the Kenyan side and disruption at the crossing. Britain advises vehicles crossing at Moyale to stay on the main A2 road and drive on to the next major town before stopping. Its Ethiopia advice, also updated, says avoid all travel within ten kilometers of the Kenyan border, except the Moyale road and town, where it advises against all but essential travel.

Reported: the utility says about ninety percent of affected customers were reconnected, and it suspects deliberate interference, without naming anyone or giving evidence.
